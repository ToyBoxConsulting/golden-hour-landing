About the Evening

When the sun softened, we showed up.

August is National Black Business Month. On August 23, as the sun came down over the Eastside, the light softened, the air warmed, and people slowed down and showed up for one another.

Golden Hour Unboxed was a free, cross-cultural evening at Bellevue Botanical Garden where Black entrepreneurs, artists, families, and allies gathered under the sunset to mingle, share, and rise together. It celebrated the many ways our community contributes to the Eastside — through art, food, storytelling, mentorship, and the everyday work of building something from nothing.

It was more than an event. Emerging Black entrepreneurs met seasoned professionals. Families experienced cross-cultural exchange in real time. Artists, vendors, and community builders were seen and supported.

This page is the record of that night. Every vendor is still linked to their shop, because an evening like this is measured by what people carry out of it.

Why This Event

Bellevue is one of Washington's most diverse cities. Intentional spaces for Black entrepreneurs to be celebrated and seen by their neighbors are still rare on the Eastside.

Golden Hour Unboxed is a free, public evening built to close that gap — for Black entrepreneurs, their families, and the whole Eastside community that gathers around them.

We hold this gathering in August, during National Black Business Month, because the timing matters. We hold it at Bellevue Botanical Garden because the place matters. We hold it as part of the City of Bellevue's Cross-Cultural Center without Walls because the structure of belonging matters.

This isn't a passive cultural showcase. It's an active exchange — Black entrepreneurs, allies, and neighbors sharing names, ideas, and how to find each other after the sun sets.

“Keep the Receipts” · Melissa Margain

Commissioned spoken word · 6:56

Every entrepreneur has receipts, and not just the ones printed on paper. Melissa wrote and performed a commissioned piece, and donated her time. It is about the unseen investments behind every dream — the sacrifices, the setbacks, and the quiet acts of faith that never make the highlight reel.

An author, poet and speaker who collects stories the way some people collect passport stamps, she is drawn to the beautifully awkward, deeply human moments we usually keep to ourselves. A U.S. Army veteran with a career in public service, she is as comfortable in a boardroom as on an open mic stage.

All night, no schedule required

Twenty tables. Black-owned businesses and makers selling inside, community organizations out in the garden. Everything bought went straight to the person who made it — and every shop is still linked below.

Pack the Box, our school supply drive, ran all evening and raised several hundred dollars in supplies and cash for the schools with the highest need.

The Black Hair Care Drive, led by Identity Unboxed and the Black Roots Coalition and hosted at the Zara Jo Beauty Supply table. Hair care products for young people across the county, in its third year.

What the Drives Collected

Two drives.
One whole evening.

Pack the Box raised several hundred dollars in school supplies and cash gifts for students in the schools with the highest need, PreK through high school.

The Black Hair Care Drive, led by Identity Unboxed and the Black Roots Coalition and hosted at the Zara Jo Beauty Supply table, collected hair care products for young people across the county, donated to Treehouse.

Gifts to Golden Hour are received through Eastside For All, a DBA of Alliance for a Just Society, a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization.
