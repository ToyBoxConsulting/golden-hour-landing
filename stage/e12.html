One page, then the whole thing

The City invested $10,000. Another $40,306 was raised or contributed alongside it, most of it donated professional time — $4.03 for every City dollar. Seventy-one and a half percent of the City’s money went to direct delivery.

It was free to every guest. Between 130 and 150 people came through. Twenty vendor and community tables filled the garden.

Two corrections for the record

Issued because this report is a public record. Both are corrected on the pages above; the filed PDF is otherwise unchanged.

Section 3 — Programme delivered
The filed text refers to “Storytellers” in the plural. That’s incorrect. One Storyteller for the Convening was introduced and performed: Chezik Tsunoda, founder of No More Under. More than one was programmed; one appeared. Every public-facing record now reads in the singular.
Section 12 — Attachments, by link
Several URLs didn’t render in the filed PDF. The Converge Media address is truncated, a stray character follows the archive line, and the King County, Primo’s Bellevue and TikTok entries carry no address at all. The complete, working list is set out on the report page and is permanently maintained there.
 

Two proclamations

King County Executive Girmay Zahilay issued a proclamation naming August Black Business Month, read at the event by Angela Phan, East King County Relations Manager in the King County Executive’s Office. The City of Bellevue issued a proclamation as well.

There was a commissioned spoken-word piece from Melissa Margain, a story from Chezik Tsunoda of No More Under, a pitch from Jermaine Murray for Fathers Against Human Trafficking, and a sunset toast.

What people said, unprompted

Nobody was asked for a testimonial. These are what people posted publicly on their own inside seventy-two hours, or said to Converge Media’s camera on the night.

“I’ve attended many networking events where the energy felt transactional… the environment Katoya cultivated exuded warmth and undeniable vibrancy. It felt like a big happy family coming together to support and learn from one another. Reciprocity was at the heart of the event.”

— Brad Blackburn III, MPA, Identity Unboxed

“A proclamation is a moment to acknowledge something special and uplift it. For the people who might not understand what a proclamation is — it’s very special to the people who are receiving it. It is saying: I see you, I’m celebrating you, and I proclaim on this day, or this month, that it’s something special.”

— Angela Phan, East King County Relations Manager, King County Executive’s Office — on camera with Converge Media

“Had a fabulous time building community and supporting local Black businesses. Many golden moments unfolded. We can’t wait to be there next year… We need to do more than fund events like this. It is about building relationships and creating pathways.”

— SPLASHForward — I sit on their board, so weigh it accordingly
